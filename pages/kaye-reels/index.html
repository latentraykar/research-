The playbook in one paragraph

She is an AI creator who casts herself (and her cat Bob) as the lead in every reel. Two machines run side by side: view machines (silent or song-driven cinematic shorts, recurring characters, borrowed fame: Billie Eilish, Red Bull, Hogwarts, Gwen Stacy) and comment machines (spoken tutorials, build breakdowns and AI news explainers). Every reel ends on one themed keyword ("comment CONCERT for prompts") that turns the comment box into a DM funnel. She posts 2-3 times a day, often ships the same clip 2-6 times within hours with a different caption, and keeps whichever one the algorithm picks.

Hook formulas

  1. Identity POV / wish fulfilment: "POV: you were born to be [character]", "Back to Hogwarts", "What if I were a movie actress?" Puts the viewer inside a fantasy in 1 line. Gwen Stacy 10.1M, Hogwarts 2.0M.
  2. Invitation into her world: "Welcome to my world", "Come visit my fantasy world", "My little island". A promise of a tour. Fantasy world 27.9M (pinned), Welcome to my world 2.2M + became a series.
  3. Emotion label over a silent visual: "The hardest goodbye", "My best friend", "This breaks my heart". The caption tells you how to feel before frame 2. Hardest goodbye 2.4M.
  4. Series promise with a stake: "I'm making commercials for brands until they notice me. Episode 2." Creates a reason to follow and come back.
  5. News contrast: "Something just happened in the world of AI. While [X warns], [Y] just launched [Z], and wait till you see how it works."
  6. Shock / mock outrage: "Hear me out, I hate ChatGPT", "[model] is going absolutely crazy". Opinion first, demo second.
  7. Borrowed fame: a mega song, brand or franchise in the first frame, plus "tag @brand in the comments". Red Bull 3.4M, Birds of a Feather 1.7M.
  8. Recurring characters: Kaye & Bob, Kitty & Bob, numbered episodes. Familiar faces lower the swipe rate.
  9. Retention bait: "Watch till the end..." on the 2.35M concert reel.

Comment-keyword CTA

  • Hit rate: 99 of 100 reels carry a single-word keyword ("CONCERT", "RAINDANCE", "TENNIS"). The only exception is the 1M-followers thank-you post.
  • Keyword is themed to the reel, not generic, so each comment also reads as a reaction. 40+ distinct keywords in 40 days.
  • The offer ladder: "prompts" (84 reels), "prompts and guide" (18), "tutorial" (12), "link" or "YouTube breakdown" (14). Bigger promise, more comments: tutorial offers pull 6.4 comments per 1k views, link offers 5.2, prompts-only 3.5.
  • Spoken CTA in the last 2 seconds on every voice-over reel ("Drop Red Bull in the comments and I'll send it over").
  • Brand ads add a second ask: "tag @redbull in the comments" (7 reels). Tags count as comments and reach the brand.
  • Tutorial and tool-demo reels hit 15-27 comments per 1k (FLY, ASTRA, Storm), 4-7x her median. The cinematic reels win views, the tutorials win comments.

Posting cadence

  • 97 posts in 40 days (Aug 30 to Oct 8): ~17 a week, on 36 of 40 days. Weekly counts: 23, 8, 12, 19, 18, 16.
  • About a quarter are re-posts of the same clip, so ~72 unique ideas: ~12-13 new ideas a week, 2 a day.
  • Days: Thursday (24) and Wednesday (20) are the heaviest, Monday (8) and Sunday (9) the lightest.
  • Times (IST): two windows. 23:00-03:00 (US lunch to afternoon) and 06:00-07:30 (US evening prime time, about 9pm ET). She posts for a US audience and almost never in the Indian daytime.

The re-post test (her quiet edge)

8 times in 40 days she posted the same clip 2-6 times within 3 hours, changing only the caption, cut or cover. The algorithm picked one winner and the rest died:

  • Demon Slayer x6: 249k vs five cuts at 200-1.3k
  • Concert x4: 278k and 223k vs 10k and 957
  • Tennis goodbye x5: 328k and 322k vs 7.5k, 1.5k, 608
  • Billie "yellow" x3: 785k vs 251k vs 21k
  • Red Bull reposts x3: 914k vs 65k vs 39k
  • Recorder x4, Cute x2, Bed x5 (all five dead at under 300)

The losers stay up. Takeaway: cheap A/B testing in public. One render, several hooks, keep the winner.
