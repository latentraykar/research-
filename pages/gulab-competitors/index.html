The short version

  • The canvas is no longer our moat. Higgsfield, Krea, Magnific (Freepik), FLORA and Figma Weave all ship node canvases, and Higgsfield and Krea also ship MCP and plugins.
  • Gulab still wins on price and on sound: the cheapest way to make a full film with voice, music and sfx in one place.
  • Gulab loses on things a filmmaker feels on day one: no camera/lens director layer, no character training, no team seats, no own model.

Where Gulab wins

  1. Price floor. $10/mo is the lowest paid entry in this set, tied with Pika. Higgsfield starts at $19, Luma at $30, Krea at $35.
  2. One canvas for a whole film with sound. Voice, voice clone, voice change, dubbing, music, sfx, lipsync, 3D and LLM nodes sit next to image and video. Most rivals have two or three of these, not all.
  3. Agent and pro-tool reach. Public API with gsk_ keys, remote MCP (21 tools), live ChatGPT and Claude connectors, Premiere and Figma plugins. Only Krea and Higgsfield match this spread.
  4. Model breadth without lock-in. Veo 3.1, Sora 2, Seedance 2.5, Wan 3.0, MiniMax H3, Nano Banana, GPT Image 2 under one credit balance.
  5. Timeline editor inside the same project, so the canvas output does not leave for CapCut.

Where Gulab is behind (gaps a filmmaker feels)

  1. No own model. Higgsfield (Soul, DoP), Runway (Gen-4.5, Aleph), Luma (Ray3), Krea (Krea 2), Pika, Kling, Hailuo, Hedra all have something only they can sell. Gulab's catalog can be copied by any reseller in a week.
  2. No packaged 'director' layer. Higgsfield Cinema Studio gives lens, light and camera presets plus cast and locations; Luma gives 16 keyframes per clip. On Gulab a filmmaker writes this into prompts.
  3. No character training. Krea and OpenArt let you train your own face or style (LoRA, personal models). Gulab has Elements, but no training on your own images was found in the code.
  4. No team workspace. FLORA, Higgsfield Canvas, Magnific Spaces, Weave and Krea all sell seats, shared pools or real-time co-editing. Nothing like it found in Gulab's code on Oct 12.
  5. No pro finishing outputs. Runway Max gives HDR, ProRes and image sequences; Luma gives HDR and EXR. A colourist cannot take a Gulab file into a grade the same way.
  6. Thin long-form talking characters. Hedra Character 3 does up to 10 minutes from audio; Gulab has one lipsync model.
  7. No distribution engine. Higgsfield pays creators per format, Leonardo has Canva, Weave has Figma, Krea claims 40M. Gulab's referral (Bloom, $5 each) is small next to these.

5 moves for Gulab

1. Ship a 'Director' preset node

Camera move, lens, light and shot size as one dropdown node that writes the prompt for each model. This is Higgsfield's Cinema Studio advantage, and it is a prompt layer, not a model. Cheapest gap to close.

2. Character lock via training

Add LoRA or reference-train on your own character (fal has trainers) and make it a canvas node. Krea and OpenArt sell this; filmmakers ask for one face across 10 shots before anything else.

3. Price the film, not the credit

Show the dollar cost of a full 30s film on the canvas before you run it, like FLORA's per-generation dollar view. At $10 entry, a 'your film costs $3.40' line beats every rival's credit maths.

4. Team seats on the same canvas

Shared project + pooled credits + per-member caps. FLORA, Weave and Higgsfield charge per seat for this; it is how agencies buy.

5. One signature move, launched as a drop

Pick one thing only Gulab does end to end (a full film with VO, music and sfx from one sentence) and push it through 10 creators in the same 4-hour window, Higgsfield style, with paid posts disclosed.
