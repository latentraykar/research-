How this was built (read first)

  • Corpus. Every ad is the official upload, mostly from Moonshot's own YouTube channel, where the agency posts its work as "Moonshot | Brand". Every link was checked live. Each ad also has a separate attribution link: trade press naming the writers, or Tanmay's own post.
  • "Tanmay" means a team. Nearly every credit reads Tanmay Bhat, Devaiah Bopanna, Puneet Chadha, Deep Joshi (sometimes Nupur Pai). None of these ads is credited to Tanmay alone. We treat "Tanmay's style" as this writers' room's style, led by him and Bopanna. Two ads (CRED 2020/2021) and Lenskart predate Moonshot's July 2023 launch.
  • Transcripts. YouTube captions couldn't be downloaded, so each ad was transcribed from the video by two independent AI listening passes, then compared word by word. Plain lines were heard the same by both passes. Shaded lines were heard by only one pass. Red lines are disputed and marked [unclear]. Where public YouTube caption text existed (CRED Dravid, CRED Warner) it matched the opening lines.
  • Visual descriptions come from the same AI pass over the video. Watch the clip before quoting a visual detail.
  • Tanmay's own words come from his Masters' Union advertising masterclass (video, published May 2023, AI-transcribed; each quote links to its timestamp) and from Devaiah Bopanna's published interviews (text transcripts). Bopanna quoting Tanmay is marked as such.

1. CRED: Bappi Lahiri "Audition"

Sep 2020 · Digital film (IPL-season campaign) · 0:50 (agency cut) · Cast: Bappi Lahiri + a 3-person ad-agency panel · watch · proof: 1 · 2 · FULL

Written pre-Moonshot (Moonshot launched Jul 2023) by Tanmay's AIB-era writing team. Moonshot's channel carries it as its own work.

1. The brief
Real brief (per afaqs): CRED was running an IPL-season "auditions" series, framed as CRED still trying to make its IPL ad. Our inference: make a credit-card bill app feel fun and exclusive ("not everyone gets it").
2. Core idea
A disco legend auditions a love-song jingle for a fintech app, and the ad panel can't get rid of him.
3. Insight
Everyone knows the cringe of brand jingles. Everyone also knows Bappi Lahiri only does one thing, and does it all the way.
4. Hook (0-5s)
0-5s: a corkboard sign "CRED Auditions This Way", then Bappi, in full gold, points at camera and sings "Baby you are my CVV. I am your OTP." The first lyric is the joke: banking jargon as a love song.
5. Structure
Song verse 1 (the gag) → panel reaction ("Seriously?") → panel cuts to a flat product voiceover (the actual ad) → Bappi keeps singing as the panel walks out → lights go off, he's still singing alone.
6. Comedy mechanics
Self-aware jab at advertising itself (the ad shows its own rejected idea); incongruent casting; committing to the gag (a full song with real rhyme scheme); a callback button ("Download CRED baby" in the dark).
7. Language & voice
English lyrics with one Hindi film idiom ("zora zori"); the humour lives in fintech words (CVV, OTP, credit limit, default, cashback, late payment fine) forced into love-song lines.
8. Product entry
Twice: inside the joke (every lyric is a feature) and plainly via the panel's "simple voiceover", so the ad sells straight while mocking selling.
9. Format & casting
Celebrity parody-in-a-sketch; the 'audition' frame lets the star be absurd while the product gets a clean VO read.
10. Ending
"Download CRED baby", sung to an empty dark room. The rejected jingle becomes the one you remember.
11. The "Tanmay" choice
The ad includes its own rejection. It knows the jingle is bad, and that knowing is the joke.

2. CRED: Rahul Dravid "Indiranagar ka Gunda"

Apr 2021 · Digital + IPL 2021 · 0:27 (agency cut) · Cast: Rahul Dravid; a suited presenter (not named in the ad) · watch · proof: 1 · 2 · FULL*

Pre-Moonshot. Opening lines confirmed by YouTube's public caption text. Two lines inside the rant are disputed between passes and marked [unclear].

1. The brief
Real, per the writer: the campaign idea was "As crazy as __" / "As unlikely as __" (Bopanna). The brand problem (inference): make 'rewards for paying bills' sound believable by admitting it sounds unbelievable.
2. Core idea
CRED rewards sound as unlikely as Rahul Dravid having anger issues, so here is Rahul Dravid with anger issues.
3. Insight
Bengaluru traffic makes anyone snap, even the calmest man in Indian cricket. Dravid's public image (the Wall, Mr Dependable) is the setup, and the audience supplies it.
4. Hook (0-5s)
0-5s: a presenter explains the product plainly, then says "I know this sounds ridiculous. It's like saying Rahul Dravid has anger issues." The product claim is the setup line.
5. Structure
Claim (0:00-0:05) → analogy (0:06) → hard cut to Dravid in traffic → 3-4 beats of escalation (shouting, the bat, the mirror) → the peak line from the sunroof → a local bystander tag.
6. Comedy mechanics
Subverted persona (the purest 'X expected, Y happens'); escalation of one gag; hyper-local specificity (Indiranagar, a Bengaluru traffic jam).
7. Language & voice
Presenter in clean English; Dravid's rant is Bengaluru road English ("Come man, come... I'll smack you!"), peaking on one Hindi-film villain line: "Indiranagar ka gunda hoon main!"
8. Product entry
The first 5 seconds only. After the analogy, the rest of the film is pure proof-by-absurdity, with no product.
9. Format & casting
Presenter-to-camera + celebrity sketch. The presenter makes the gag 'legal' as a product claim.
10. Ending
"Indiranagar ka gunda hoon main!", a line people repeated for years, then the "great for the good" end card.
11. The "Tanmay" choice
The product claim itself is the setup, and the celebrity is the punchline.

3. Lenskart: "The War of Fair Prices" ft. Karan Johar & Peyush Bansal

2023 (before Jul 2023) · Digital film · 0:44 · Cast: Karan Johar, Peyush Bansal (Lenskart founder) · watch · proof: 1 · 2 · FULL

Predates Moonshot's public launch; credited to Tanmay as a script. Exact release month not confirmed.

1. The brief
Inference, backed by the on-screen supers "Factory Wale Prices / NO MIDDLEMEN": explain why premium-looking frames cost ₹999, without looking cheap.
2. Core idea
A rich celebrity tries to bargain a price UP because ₹999 is beneath him.
3. Insight
In India low price = doubt about quality, and status = paying more. Bargaining is a national sport. Flip its direction.
4. Hook (0-5s)
0-5s: Karan at a posh café calls a sleeping Peyush: "I was just on Lenskart... amazing designs." Then: "Lekin ye jo price hai 999, dollars, right?"
5. Structure
Call → mistaken currency → the reverse haggle ("Price thoda zyada ho sakta hai kya?") → the 'because' line ("It's only because we don't have middlemen") → Karan offers to FUND middlemen → counter-offers going down (90,000... "na tera na mera, 80,000") → exit curse.
6. Comedy mechanics
Reversal of a universal ritual (reverse bargaining); persona play (Karan's luxury image); a founder as deadpan straight man; a callback to haggling idiom ("na tera na mera").
7. Language & voice
Fast Hinglish phone talk; bazaar idioms in a luxury mouth ("Ameero ki haaye lagegi tujhe").
8. Product entry
The price is the whole joke, and the reason (no middlemen) is delivered by the founder as a line of dialogue, not a VO.
9. Format & casting
Two-hander phone sketch: celebrity + real founder.
10. Ending
"Ameero ki haaye lagegi tujhe, we don't want that.", then VO: "Factory wale prices, no middlemen."
11. The "Tanmay" choice
The joke IS the value proposition, just turned upside down.

Moonshot's launch film.

Subway's build-your-own counter is so complex that even a five-time world chess champion treats it like a match, and loses.

6. Comedy mechanics
Extended metaphor (chess jargon mapped onto sandwich choices); escalation in threes; persona play (genius overwhelmed); brand self-deprecation.
7. Language & voice
Inner monologue in chess English with Hindi grammar glue ("Mayo capture karoon toh red chilli gets compromised").
8. Product entry
The whole problem is Subway's own menu; the solution arrives as "Isliye" ("that's why") at ~0:22.
9. Format & casting
Celebrity inner-monologue sketch.
10. Ending
Calm bite of a Hotseller + super "Ordering made super simple".
11. The "Tanmay" choice

What nearly all of them share (true across most of the corpus)

  1. The product claim is the joke's engine, not a sign-off. In 13 of 15 the claim is the setup or the punchline. You can't retell the joke without the product.

  2. One incongruence: X is expected, Y happens. Every ad has exactly one "wrong" thing at its centre, and everything else is played straight.

4. Step 3: the method, from brief to script

  1. Reduce the brief to one claim.
    The question he asks: What is the one thing this product does, in under 10 words?
    Example: Lenskart: "no middlemen, so ₹999". Everything in the film serves that line.
  2. Find where that claim lives in real Indian life.
    The question he asks: Where does this pain or this benefit actually happen? What is the trope of that moment?
    Example: MakeMyTrip: overspend on the hotel, then haggle over the fun part.
  3. Break the trope with one incongruence.
    The question he asks: What is expected here, and what's the most surprising thing that could happen instead?
    Example: Vadilal: Mars landing expected to get the "Waah!"; the ice cream gets it.
  4. Cast against perception, or cast a pun.
    The question he asks: Which famous face makes this incongruence funnier because of what people already think of them?
    Example: CRED: the calmest man in cricket, in road rage. Instamart: the surname is the pun.
  5. Pick a format everyone knows and commit to it.
    The question he asks: Which genre or ritual can I copy exactly, so the audience knows the rules before line 1?
    Example: Bold Care: a complete 90s teleshopping show. Shark Tank: a full bidaai.
  6. Write line 1 as a plain statement of the situation.
    The question he asks: Can someone scrolling understand the scene from the first sentence?
    Example: OYO: "Papa, Rishabh aur maine weekend pe OYO book kiya hai."
  7. Escalate in three specific beats.
    The question he asks: What are three steps, each more specific and more absurd than the last?
    Example: Subway: bread → veggies → "condiment checkmate".
  8. Put the product in as the "because".
    The question he asks: Who in the scene can say why this works, as dialogue?
    Example: Meesho: "Sir, Meesho seedha factory se bhijwata hai. It's possible."
  9. Give a straight man the last word, or add a button.
    The question he asks: After the product line, is there one more laugh that re-states the joke?
    Example: MakeMyTrip: "Aa jayega wapas."
  10. Let the celebrity read the benefit straight, then cut.
    The question he asks: Have we earned enough attention to spend 5 seconds on a clean product read?
    Example: Muthoot: "Bas ek missed call and book your Gold Loan."
  11. Rewrite until the gag lands by 0:35.
    The question he asks: Does the product turn land by 0:35? What can I cut?
    Example: Tanmay's process: a vomit draft, then "do round edit... minimum" (29:47). 12 of 15 ads turn by 0:35.

The 5 mistakes that make an imitation feel fake

  1. A joke that works without the product (a sketch with a logo stuck on). Fix: make the claim the setup or the punchline.
  2. Hinglish as flavour instead of a register switch. Random "yaar"s aren't the style. Fix: collide two worlds of vocabulary (corporate/filmy, chess/sandwich, Doordarshan/taboo).
  3. A celebrity as a face, or cast for no reason. Fix: the joke must depend on what the audience already thinks of that person.
  4. Too many gags. Several unrelated jokes read like a stand-up set. Fix: one incongruence, escalated in three beats.
  5. A sincere "heart" ending or a real jingle. Fix: end on a repeatable line or a button, and parody the jingle if you must have one.
