How to use this

Work the levels, not the listEvery resource carries a tag: Beginner, Intermediate or Advanced. Clear the Beginner tier of a track before you touch Advanced. Depth beats a hoard of bookmarks.
Watch like an editorWatch every film twice: once for feeling, once with the sound off and a notebook. Count the cuts in a scene, name the shot sizes, note where the light comes from.
Make something every weekEvery track ends in exercises. One finished small piece a week teaches more than ten unfinished big ones.
Steal the structure, not the surfaceBreak down what a great scene does (the turn, the reveal, the rhythm), then rebuild that function in your own world. That is how every great director in this guide learned.
01

Directing & visual storytelling

The core job: turning an idea into images and moments that make people feel something. Everything else on this page feeds into this track.

Core principles

Every shot answers a questionWhat does the audience need to know or feel right now? If a shot answers nothing, cut it.
Blocking is storytellingWhere people stand, who moves and who stays still, shows power and emotion before a word is spoken.
Point of viewDecide whose story each scene is. The camera sits with that person.
Contrast drives interestBig after small, loud after quiet, fast after slow. Sameness kills attention.
The idea over the executionA clear idea shot on a phone beats a vague one shot on an ARRI. Creative direction is taste plus clarity.

Practice

Beginner
  • Recreate one 30s scene from a film you love, shot for shot, on your phone.
  • Tell a 60s story with no dialogue, 6 shots max.
Intermediate

  • Write and shoot a 2-minute short with one location and two actors.
Advanced

  • Write a director's treatment (look, sound, casting, references) for a brand you admire and pitch it to a friend as if it were real.
02

Camera & cinematography

Light, lens, frame and movement. The visual grammar every creative director must speak fluently with a DP.

Core principles

Exposure triangleAperture (depth of field), shutter (motion blur, keep near 180 degrees) and ISO (noise). Know what each one costs you.
Lens = emotionWide lenses put you in the space and distort faces up close; long lenses compress and isolate. Pick the lens for the feeling, not the framing.
Shot sizes & anglesECU to extreme wide, eye level to high and low. Each size has a job: information, emotion or geography.
CompositionRule of thirds, then learn to break it: centre framing, negative space, leading lines, frames within frames, depth layers.
Motivated lightEvery light should feel like it comes from somewhere: a window, a lamp, a screen. Key, fill, back, then learn soft vs hard and contrast ratio.
Camera movement means somethingA move should reveal, follow or change our relationship with a character. No move without a reason.
ColourPalette, contrast and grading shape mood. Learn log footage, LUTs and basic grading in DaVinci Resolve.

03

Editing

The last rewrite of the film. Editing decides rhythm, meaning and emotion; most creative direction is won or lost here.

Core principles

Murch's rule of sixEmotion first, then story, rhythm, eye-trace, 2D plane, 3D space. When in doubt, cut for emotion.
Cut on action & on the lookCuts hide inside movement and eyelines. A look off-screen asks for the next shot.
Kuleshov effectMeaning lives between two shots. The same face means different things after different images.
Rhythm & pacingShot length is music. Vary it on purpose; slow down for weight, speed up for energy.
J and L cutsLet sound lead or trail the picture. It is the fastest way to make a cut feel invisible.
Kill your darlingsThe best shot you have may not belong. Serve the whole, not the moment.

04

Sound design & music

Half the film. Audiences forgive bad images far more than bad sound, and sound is where cheap work looks expensive.

Core principles

LayersDialogue, ambience/room tone, Foley, hard effects, music. Build each layer on its own track.
Diegetic vs non-diegeticSound in the world vs sound for the audience. Crossing that line on purpose is a powerful tool.
Silence is a soundDrop everything before the big moment. Contrast makes the hit land.
Sound leads pictureStart the next scene's sound before its image to pull the audience forward.
Record clean firstMic close, room quiet, 30s of room tone every location. You cannot fix bad sound in the mix.
Music is a characterUse score to add what the picture does not say, never to repeat it.

05

Scriptwriting & story

Story is the thing clients buy and audiences remember. Write short, write often, and read real scripts before you read more theory.

Core principles

Want vs needA character chases what they want and ends up with what they need. That gap is your story.
Conflict is the engineNo obstacle, no scene. Every scene needs something someone wants and something in the way.
StructureThree acts, the sequence method, the hero's journey, Save the Cat beats. Learn them all, then use them as checklists, not formulas.
Show, don't tellAction and image carry meaning. Dialogue is what people say while avoiding what they mean (subtext).
Enter late, leave earlyStart a scene as late as possible and cut out as soon as its point lands.
The rewrite is the writingFirst drafts are for you. Every draft after that is for the audience.
Ads are tiny filmsA 30s spot still needs a hook, a turn and a payoff. One idea, clearly told.

06

Advertising & creative direction

Where most creative directors earn their name: one clear idea, crafted to the last frame, that moves people and sells. Pair this with the Piyush Pandey and Tanmay Bhat frameworks already on your hub.

Core principles

One idea, one sentenceIf you cannot say the idea in one line, it is not ready to shoot.
Insight before executionFind the human truth first (what people feel but don't say), then the product's role in it.
Earn the first 2 secondsOnline, the hook is everything. Open on tension, surprise or a question.
Brand in the storyThe product should be the turn of the story, not a logo stuck on the end.
Craft is the multiplierCasting, sound and edit decide whether a good idea becomes a great ad.
Kill most of your ideasGreat CDs write 50 ideas to find one. Volume first, taste second.

07

Documentary

Real people, real stakes, story found in the edit. Documentary trains the instincts every creative director needs: listening, patience and finding structure in chaos.

Core principles

Access is everythingThe story you get depends on the trust you build. Spend time before you roll.
Character over topicNobody cares about an issue; they care about a person living it.
Observational vs participatoryFly on the wall (Wiseman) or the filmmaker in the film (Herzog, Moore). Choose your stance.
The story is found in the editShoot widely, log everything, build a paper edit, then find the spine.
EthicsConsent, representation and what you owe your subjects. Decide your lines before the shoot.
InterviewsAsk open questions, stay silent after the answer, the best line comes after the pause.

Separate wing

The comedy wing

Comedy is a craft with rules, not a gift. It is structure, truth and timing, and the fastest way to learn it is to write a lot and perform or post it. This wing is its own full course.

The course

1. What makes things funny

SurpriseA joke sets up an expectation and breaks it. The setup builds an assumption; the punchline reveals a second, unexpected meaning (Greg Dean's target assumption).
TruthThe biggest laughs come from recognition: 'that is so true'. Observe what people do but never say.
Benign violationSomething feels wrong, but safe. Too safe is boring, too wrong is offensive; comedy lives on the edge between.
StatusWatching the high get brought low, or the low act high. Most sketches are status games.
Comic perspectiveEvery funny character sees the world in a specific, flawed way and never changes it (John Vorhaus).

2. Joke craft

Setup and punchSetup: shortest path to the assumption. Punch: the reveal word as late as possible, ideally the last word.
Rule of threeTwo items set a pattern, the third breaks it.
MisdirectionLead the audience down one meaning, pay off another.
TagsExtra punchlines after the first, no new setup. Where pros make a joke 3 laughs long.
CallbacksBring back an earlier joke in a new context. Rewards attention, builds a set into a whole.
Act-outsShow, don't tell: play the character instead of describing them.
EconomyCut every word you can. Funny dies in extra syllables.

3. Stand-up

Premise firstStart with an opinion or attitude about a topic (weird, hard, stupid, scary), not with a joke.
Write what's true for youYour specific life is your material. Nobody else has it.
Build a tight fiveFive minutes of your strongest bits. That is the currency of open mics and auditions.
Record every setListen back, mark every laugh, cut what didn't land, rewrite, try again.
Stage timeThere is no substitute. Aim for 2-3 open mics a week once you have a set.

4. Sketch & improv

Find the gameThe first unusual thing in a scene is the game. Heighten it, explore it, repeat it (UCB method).
Yes, andAccept the reality and add to it. Blocking kills scenes.
HeighteningEach beat pushes the game further than the last, until it can't go further. Then button it.
Grounded charactersThe funniest sketch characters believe their strange logic completely.
Premise to pitchWrite the premise in one line: 'What if X happened in Y?' If the line is funny, the sketch can be.

5. Visual & screen comedy

Frame the jokeEdgar Wright style: jokes live in the frame, the edit and the sound, not just the dialogue.
Timing is editingHold a beat longer than comfortable, or cut the instant the joke lands. Both are choices.
RevealsHide information off-frame and reveal it with a pan, a cut or a zoom.
DeadpanPlay it straight. The less the actor sells the joke, the funnier it usually is.
Comedy in adsThe funniest ads make the product the punchline or the reason for the joke, never a side note.

The AI filmmaking wing

AI does not replace the craft you are learning above: it rewards it. A prompt is a shot brief. The people winning here are the ones who already think in shot sizes, light, beats and cuts. This wing is its own course: how to prompt, why prompts work or fail, the official guide for every major model, the films to study, and who to follow.

How to prompt better

Brief a cinematographerWrite as if briefing a DP who has never seen your storyboard: shot size, lens, light, what happens. Anything you leave out, the model decides for you. (OpenAI, Sora 2 guide)
Nouns and verbs you can see"Beautiful" and "cinematic" are not pictures. Wet asphalt, a zebra crossing and neon in puddles are. (Sora 2 guide)
One camera move, one actionEach shot gets one clear camera move and one clear subject action. Two moves in 5 seconds is how you get mush. (Sora 2 guide)
Time the action in beatsCount it out: four steps, a pause, the curtain pulled in the final second. Beats make motion believable. (Sora 2 guide)
Lock a paletteName 3 to 5 colours and the light sources. That is what makes shots from different generations cut together. (Sora 2 guide)
Use the formulaSubject + movement + scene + camera language + lighting. Kling publishes it as its prompt formula; it works on almost every model.
Image-to-video: prompt motion onlyYour start frame already sets composition, subject, light and style. Re-describing them fights the image. Describe what moves. (Runway image-to-video guide)
Dialogue in quotes, sound on its own lineModels with native audio read lines in quotation marks as speech and an Audio: line as the sound design. (Google DeepMind, Veo 3 guide)
Same words, same characterSmall wording changes alter a face or costume. Reuse the exact character description in every shot. (Sora 2 guide)
Change one thing per retry"Same shot, switch to 85 mm." Iterate like a director giving one note at a time, never re-roll everything. (Sora 2 guide)

Prompt breakdowns: why these work

Weak
A beautiful street at night
Strong
Wet asphalt, zebra crosswalk, neon signs reflecting in puddles

Why it works: The weak line is a judgement, not an image, so the model averages every street it has seen. The strong line lists three things a camera can actually record, and the reflections give it free light and depth.

Quoted verbatim from OpenAI Sora 2 Prompting Guide ↗
Weak
Person moves quickly
Strong
Cyclist pedals three times, brakes, and stops at crosswalk

Why it works: "Quickly" has no start or end. Three pedal strokes, a brake and a stop is a beat sheet the model can pace across the clip, and it gives your edit a clean out-point.

Quoted verbatim from OpenAI Sora 2 Prompting Guide ↗
Weak
Cinematic look
Strong
Anamorphic 2.0x lens, shallow DOF, volumetric light

Why it works: "Cinematic" means nothing specific. A lens, a depth of field and a light behaviour are real camera decisions: this is where your camera track pays off.

Quoted verbatim from OpenAI Sora 2 Prompting Guide ↗
Weak
Actor walks across the room.
Strong
Actor takes four steps to the window, pauses, and pulls the curtain in the final second.

Why it works: Counted steps, a pause and an action pinned to the final second turn a vague move into blocking. It is exactly how you would direct an actor on set.

Quoted verbatim from OpenAI Sora 2 Prompting Guide ↗
Prompt
A medium shot frames an old sailor, his knitted blue sailor hat casting a shadow over his eyes, a thick grey beard obscuring his chin. He holds his pipe in one hand, gesturing with it towards the churning, grey sea beyond the ship's railing. "This ocean, it's a force, a wild, untamed might. And she commands your awe, with every breaking light"

Why it works: Shot size comes first, then two wardrobe details that also do lighting work (the hat's shadow), one prop gesture that points the eye to the background, and the line in quotes so the model voices it. One subject, one action, one line.

Quoted verbatim from Google DeepMind, Veo 3 prompt guide ↗
Prompt
A snow-covered plain of iridescent moon-dust under twilight skies. Thirty-foot crystalline flowers bloom, refracting light into slow-moving rainbows. A fur-cloaked figure walks between these colossal blossoms, leaving the only footprints in untouched dust.

Why it works: A number gives scale (thirty-foot), the light is told how to behave (refracting into slow rainbows), and one small human figure sells the size. "The only footprints" is a story detail: the frame now says loneliness without saying it.

Quoted verbatim from Google DeepMind, Veo 3 prompt guide ↗
Prompt
Camping (Stop Motion): Camper: "I'm one with nature now!" Bear: "Nature would prefer some personal space."

Why it works: Straight from your comedy wing: a title, a style tag and a two-line setup and punchline. Short prompts leave the model room; the joke structure does the directing. Use this shape for sketch ideas.

Quoted verbatim from Google DeepMind, Veo 3 prompt guide ↗
Prompt
A keyboard whose keys are made of different types of candy. Typing makes sweet, crunchy sounds. Audio: Crunchy, sugary typing sounds, delighted giggles.

Why it works: The sound is written as its own layer. An explicit Audio: line is how you sound-design inside the prompt instead of leaving it to chance.

Quoted verbatim from Google DeepMind, Veo 3 prompt guide ↗

Why prompts fail

Adjectives with no pictureBeautiful, epic, stunning, cinematic. They describe a feeling about the shot, not the shot.
Two camera moves in one clipA push-in plus an orbit plus a tilt in five seconds. Pick one; cut to the next shot for the next move.
Too much actionFive things happening in one clip gets you warped limbs. One action, timed in beats.
Fighting the start frameIn image-to-video, re-describing a different composition or style than the image shows makes the model argue with itself.
Drifting character wording"A tall man in a coat" in shot 1 and "a man in a dark jacket" in shot 2 are two different people to the model.
Re-rolling everythingChanging five things per retry means you never learn which word did it. One note per take.

Practice

Beginner
  • Take the 4 weak prompts above, run weak and strong on the same model, put them side by side. Write one line on what changed.
  • Write 10 one-shot prompts with the formula: subject, one action in beats, scene, one camera move, light.
  • Make a 3-shot sequence with one character. Copy-paste the exact same character description into all 3.
Intermediate
  • Pick one ANERNEQ scene. Read its published prompt, regenerate it, and list 3 differences and why.
  • Image-to-video: make a start frame, then write 3 motion-only prompts for it. Keep the one that respects the image.
  • Turn a sketch from your comedy wing into a 30-second AI short using the setup/punchline prompt shape.
Advanced
  • Recreate one shot from a live-action film you love, shot for shot: shot size, lens, light, blocking. Grade the gap.
  • Make a 60-second spec ad for a real brand with a locked palette and 5+ shots that cut together.
  • Run an open model (Wan or LTX) in ComfyUI and match a closed-model shot.

Archives & reference libraries

Where you steal like an artist: frames, footage, sound, scripts and ads. Build a personal reference library from these and never start a moodboard from zero again.

Core principles

Save with a reasonTag every saved frame with why: light, palette, blocking, lens. Untagged references are noise.
Check the rightsPublic domain, Creative Commons and licensed archive are different things. Know which one you are using before you publish.
One library, many projectsKeep one searchable reference folder (Eagle, Are.na, Notion) and add to it daily.

Your plan from day 1

16 weeks, four phases. Every day runs the same core loop; every week has one focus and one thing you finish. After week 16, restart at the Advanced tier of each track.

Every day (about 2 hours)

30 minWatch & break downOne scene or ad with a notebook: shot sizes, cuts, sound, the turn.
30 minReadOne chapter from the current track's book.
30-60 minMakeThe week's exercise. Shoot, cut, write or record something every day.
15 minComedy reps10 premises or 3 jokes. Every day, no exceptions.
5 minReferenceSave 5 frames to your library, each tagged with one craft reason.

Week by week

Phase 1: FoundationsWeeks 1-4 · Learn the grammar.
Week 1
Directing + comedy basics

Read Katz ch. 1-3 and The Comic Toolbox part 1. Watch 5 Every Frame a Painting essays. Shoot a 6-shot silent story. Set up Letterboxd and your reference library.

Week 2
Camera

Mascelli, Five C's. Shoot 7 shot sizes and 3 angles. Light a face with one lamp, 5 setups. Save 35 tagged frames from Film-Grab.

Week 3
Editing

Read In the Blink of an Eye. Cut the same footage 3 ways (sad, funny, tense). Download DaVinci Resolve, finish the official beginner course.

Week 4
Sound

Sonnenschein ch. 1-4. Rebuild the sound of a 30s silent clip. Record 20 Foley sounds. First checkpoint: a 60s short with no dialogue, shot, cut and mixed by you.

Phase 2: StoryWeeks 5-8 · Learn to tell a story, and to be funny on purpose.
Week 5
Script structure

Save the Cat, then Into the Woods. Read 2 produced scripts with their films. Write one page a day.

Week 6
Comedy writing

Greg Dean's book. 3 jokes a day, 10 premises a day. Transcribe 5 minutes of a special and mark the structure.

Week 7
Ads

Hey Whipple. Break down 3 D&AD winners. Write five 30s scripts for one product.

Week 8
Make it

Write, shoot and edit a 2-3 minute short or a 45s spec ad. Show it to 3 people and take notes. Perform your first 3 minutes at an open mic.

Phase 3: VoiceWeeks 9-12 · Find what only you can make.
Week 9
Documentary

Rabiger. Watch Grizzly Man, The Thin Blue Line, All That Breathes. Shoot a 3-minute portrait of someone you know.

Week 10
Look & colour

Blain Brown's lighting chapters. Match 3 film stills. Grade your portrait film into 3 looks and write a lookbook.

Week 11
Sketch & visual comedy

UCB manual. Write 5 sketch premises; write the best as 3 pages. Shoot a 60s visual joke with no dialogue.

Week 12
Campaign

Write a full campaign for a brand you like: insight, idea, hero film, 3 social cuts, one OOH. Shoot the hero film.

Phase 4: MasteryWeeks 13-16 · Direct like a pro and ship a portfolio.
Week 13
Pre-production

Write a director's treatment and shot list for a 3-5 minute film. Cast it, recce it, storyboard it.

Week 14
Production

Shoot it with a small crew. Record clean sound and room tone. Keep a daily log.

Week 15
Post

Paper edit, assembly, fine cut, sound design, grade. Get notes from someone better than you.

Week 16
Ship

Finish and publish. Build a one-page portfolio: best film, best ad, best comedy piece, your lookbook. Tight five at an open mic, recorded. Then restart the cycle at the Advanced tier.

Day 1, tonight: make a Letterboxd account, start your reference folder, watch Every Frame a Painting on Edgar Wright's visual comedy, write 10 comedy premises, and shoot a 6-shot silent story on your phone.
