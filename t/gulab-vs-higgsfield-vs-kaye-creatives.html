Gulab vs Higgsfield vs kaye.creatives

One doc: product table vs Higgsfield, content compare vs Higgsfield and Kaye, a 14-item steal list with effort, and where Gulab already wins.

1. The verdict, on one screen

Higgsfield is a product company with a distribution machine attached. It owns a few models (Soul, DoP), wraps the rest into named tools (Cinema Studio 4.0, Marketing Studio, AI Influencer, Genjutsu, Katana, Supercomputer), and launches something on Instagram almost every weekday.

kaye.creatives is a creator brand, not a tool. One recurring face (her, plus her cat), short song-led loops, and a "comment X for the prompt" ending on almost every reel. She shows that a human lead beats a product feed for reach.

Gulab has the widest model and sound stack on one canvas, the cheapest paid entry ($10 / ₹799), and real agent and pro-tool reach (API, MCP, ChatGPT and Claude connectors, Premiere, After Effects, Figma and Blender). Two things are missing: packaging, because users still write every camera move and look into the prompt, and a content engine, because we post a few times a week with no recurring face and no keyword CTA.

The biggest lifts:

  • Product: turn the canvas into named presets and studios: a Director node, character lock and a shareable preset library.
  • Content: you as the recurring face, one Gulab feature per reel, an "order the AI" hook, a keyword CTA, 2 posts a day on weekdays.

Their best formats, from the 200-reel teardown:

  • Agent job skit, 60-75s: a person barks a job at the AI in the first 3 seconds, and a visible asset lands every 5-8 seconds. 411k median views, 14 comments per 1k (2x the account).
  • Launch walk-through, 45-70s: "X is finally here, look at this", then 5-7 escalating demos.
  • Cinematic short, 75-120s: a cold open mid-conflict, with the product only on the end card. Their #1 reel is one of these (3.6M).
  • Offer with a deadline: the highest comment rate of any angle (15 per 1k).
  • Caption rule: a headline first, with the keyword underneath. Reels that open the caption with "Comment..." do worse (250k median vs 377k).

4. Steal list

Effort: S = days, M = 1-2 weeks, L = more. Product items need Rohit's call before they're built.

Product

1. Director node (from Cinema Studio). Camera move, lens, light, shot size and era as dropdowns, and the node writes the right prompt for each model. It's a prompt layer, not a model, so it's our cheapest gap to close. Next step: draft 10 presets and propose them in Core. S-M
2. Character lock (from Soul ID). Train or reference-lock your own face or character once, then reuse it as one node across every shot. Filmmakers ask for "one face across 10 shots" first. Next step: check the trainers available on our providers and spec a node. M
3. Preset library with community presets (from Katana). Save any canvas as a preset with a 3-30s preview, share it, and let others run it with their own images. Our starter canvases are the seed. Next step: spec "save canvas as preset". M
4. Named studios as front doors (from Marketing Studio and AI Influencer). "Ad from a product link" and "build an AI influencer" as one-click canvases. This is the same canvas underneath with a clearer way in. Next step: build two starter canvases and test them on new signups. M
5. Time-boxed offers (from Unlimited Wan 3.0). One model, free or unlimited for 7 days, with a deadline. This feeds the highest-comment reel format. Next step: Rohit or Shantnu decide what we can give away. S
6. A Gulab skill for Claude (from Katana in Claude). One-photo edit presets that run through our MCP from a Claude chat. Next step: package 3 presets as a skill. M
7. Show the price of the film before the run. "This 30s film costs $3.40" on the canvas. At a $10 entry, this beats every rival's credit maths. S

Content (yours)

8. You as the recurring lead, one Gulab feature per reel. This is Kaye's face combined with Higgsfield's one-thing-per-reel rule. S
9. "Order the AI" hooks. "I need a trailer by 6pm" in line one, with an asset landing every 6 seconds. Start with the six Gulab reels in the 200-reel doc. S
10. Caption line 1 = headline with a famous name. "Meet Gulab x Seedance 2.5 for trailers", with the keyword below it. Never open the caption with "Comment". S
11. One keyword per reel, plus an auto-DM. For example, "comment STORYBOARD", and the link or prompt arrives in DMs. Your comment-to-DM research covers the tooling. M
12. A launch drop on every model day. We ship new models to prod within an hour, so post a launch walk-through reel the same day. S
13. A tentpole short every few weeks. The Acheri short, with Gulab on the end card only. This is where the 1M+ reels come from on both accounts. L
14. Cadence and testing. 2 posts a day on weekdays. Product reels go out 9am-3pm ET (6:30pm-12:30am IST), creative reels late US night. Post two edits of the same reel on the same day and keep the winner. M

5. Where Gulab already wins

  • Price floor: $10 (₹799) vs Higgsfield's $15, in INR with UPI in India, and credits roll over for 60 days.
  • A whole film with sound in one place: voice, voice clone, dubbing, music, synced sfx (Mirelo) and lipsync sit next to image and video on the same canvas. Higgsfield's audio is thin next to this.
  • Model breadth under one balance: Veo 3.1, Sora 2, Seedance 2.5 (1080p, draft mode), Wan 3.0, MiniMax H3 and Hailuo 03, Nano Banana, GPT Image 2, plus LLM, 3D and world models.
  • Pro-tool reach: Premiere, After Effects, Figma and Blender, plus MCP and the ChatGPT and Claude connectors. Higgsfield has MCP and ChatGPT, but not the editing suites.
  • Canvas plus timeline in one project: the output doesn't have to leave for CapCut.
  • Speed on new models: they go to prod within an hour of release.

Our edge is the stack. Higgsfield's edge is the packaging and the noise. The steal list closes the packaging gap, and the content list closes the noise gap.
